SfN Response Framework

Guiding the Society for Neuroscience in timely policy advocacy and public communication. This document is intended as a complement to the full SfN Statement Decision Tree document, which should be referenced first.

The Rapid Response Framework provides SfN policy staff with a structured, time-bound process for issuing organizational statements in response to events, policy developments, or crises that require timely action. This framework is not a substitute for full deliberation, but rather a complement to it—ensuring the Society can speak clearly and quickly without sacrificing our scientific integrity or governance standards.

Purpose and scope

A rapid response is appropriate when:

  • An external event or policy development requires organizational comment or clarification within days, not weeks
  • The issue is within SfN's established mission, prior positions, or scientific expertise
  • The scientific evidence on the topic is well-established and not substantially contested within our community
  • The statement can be issued without substantially revising governance, positions, or strategy
  • The response advances coalition-building, member engagement, or mission alignment without shifting the Society's public role
What this is not
  • A decision framework for whether SfN should respond (that is made elsewhere, earlier in the process)
  • A substitute for consultation with leadership, committees, or external partners
  • License to make new policy positions or expand SfN's advocacy scope
  • A shortcut for contentious issues or matters where SfN leadership disagrees on substance
  • A replacement for the full SfN advocacy process for major positions or strategy shifts
Key success metrics

A successful rapid response:

  • Is released when the moment matters (within the event window, not after news cycle has moved on)
  • Advances a specific SfN objective (member guidance, coalition support, mission clarity, or legislative influence)
  • Reflects the scientific evidence accurately and completely
  • Maintains consistency with prior SfN positions and statements
  • Receives appropriate governance sign-off before release
  • Is understood and able to be defended by spokespersons and members

Use this seven-step decision tree to determine whether a rapid response is the right tool, which tier it falls into, and what approvals are needed.

Step 1: Is this within our scope?
Question: Is the issue directly related to neuroscience research, brain health, or science policy, and does SfN have established expertise or prior positions to ground a response?
No → Stop. Route to the Executive Director for a governance decision. Do not use rapid response process.
Yes → Continue to Step 2.
Step 2: Is the timing right?
Question: Does SfN need to comment within 3–5 days to be relevant to the news cycle, policy window, or member needs?
No → Use the standard SfN position-development process instead. Rapid response is not necessary.
Yes → Continue to Step 3.
Step 3: Is the evidence settled?
Question: Is the scientific basis for this response well-established, non-controversial within neuroscience, and defensible based on published evidence?
No → Escalate to GPA Chair and senior staff. Do not proceed with rapid response unless leadership confirms the evidence is solid enough to defend publicly.
Yes → Continue to Step 4.
Step 4: Can we narrow the scope?
Question: Can the statement be limited to core SfN positions, or would it require SfN to take a new stance on a contested topic?
No → Escalate to Executive Director. This may require Executive Committee input before proceeding.
Yes → Continue to Step 5.
Step 5: Will we alienate key stakeholders?
Question: Have coalition partners, major donors, or key member constituencies been consulted? Will the statement create internal friction or external backlash that rapid response cannot handle?
No → Pause and consult. Reach out to relevant stakeholders before drafting.
Yes → Continue to Step 6.
Step 6: What tier is this?
Question: Based on visibility, controversy, legal risk, and coalition impact, which tier applies?

Tier 1 (Rapid): Low-visibility, uncontroversial comment on a topic within established SfN positions. Internal issue clarification. Technical science communication. No legal or reputational risk. Approval: Advocacy staff + GPA Chair. Timeline: 1–2 days.

Tier 2 (Standard): Moderate visibility; some controversy; potentially multi-stakeholder impact. Reflects established SfN positions but reaches new audiences. Coalition statement. Public advocacy on known issues. Low legal or reputational risk. Approval: Advocacy staff + GPA Chair + Senior Staff. Timeline: 2–3 days.

Tier 3 (Escalated): High visibility; significant controversy; major coalition implications; potential legal, financial, or reputational risk. Statement breaks new ground or represents a shift in SfN's public positioning. Major media attention expected. Approval: Advocacy staff + GPA Chair + Senior Staff + Executive Director. Timeline: 3–5 days.

Step 7: What statement type?
Question: Which format best fits our message and audience?

Official Statement: Full, on-the-record organizational position. Signed by the President or Executive Director. Suitable for policy impact, coalition statements, public advocacy, or member communication. 250–500 words.

Letter to Policymakers: Directed to specific legislators, agency officials, or other decision-makers. Urges action on a bill, regulation, or appointment. Typically 200–400 words. Can be published or held private.

Op-ed or Article: Longer-form (700–1000 words). Published in news outlet or SfN's own communication channels. Explains science, context, and SfN's view in narrative form. May feature external byline (e.g., GPA Chair).

FAQs or Fact Sheet: Internal or member-facing document. Answers anticipated questions about a topic. Explains SfN's position without claiming to be the Society's official voice on every detail. Suitable for complex or evolving situations.

Holding Statement: Minimal comment (1–2 sentences) released immediately while full statement is being developed. Signals that SfN is aware, is taking the issue seriously, and will provide more information soon. Buys time without committing to a full response.

The rapid response framework uses three tiers based on visibility, controversy, scope, and risk. Tier determines timeline, approval requirements, and review depth.

Tier 1: Rapid response 1–2 days

When to use: Low-visibility, uncontroversial statements within established SfN positions.

Examples: Technical clarification about neuroscience findings in media coverage. Statement congratulating new leadership at a neuroscience funder. Support for an educational initiative that aligns with SfN mission. Minor policy comment on a topic where SfN has clear prior positions.

Key characteristics:

  • Not expected to generate significant news coverage outside trade or science media
  • Unlikely to be controversial within or outside SfN
  • Does not introduce new SfN positions, only clarifies or reiterates established ones
  • No anticipated legal or reputational risk
  • Single stakeholder focus (not multi-coalition or multi-party)
  • Members unlikely to question or push back on SfN's participation

Approval path:

  • Advocacy staff drafts
  • GPA Chair reviews and approves (or provides feedback for revision)
  • Approval is documented in policy activity log

Senior Staff and Executive Director notification: Inform via email with 24 hours' notice before release. If either raises concerns, escalate to Tier 2.

Tier 2: Standard response 2–3 days

When to use: Moderate visibility, some controversy, or multi-stakeholder impact. Reflects established SfN positions but reaches broader audiences.

Examples: Public statement on a bill advancing through Congress related to neuroscience funding. Coalition statement on a policy change (with partner vetting). Member letter explaining SfN's position on a topic generating member questions. Media statement on a scientific finding or safety issue relevant to public policy. Statement of concern regarding a policy, decision, or action.

Key characteristics:

  • Expected to receive modest coverage in trade, science, or policy media
  • May generate some member or coalition partner questions
  • Reflects SfN's established positions, possibly applied to a new context or audience
  • Low to moderate legal or reputational risk (if any)
  • May involve coalition partners; alignment checked before release
  • Likely to strengthen SfN's visibility or impact in policy or advocacy circles
  • May include statements of concern that express SfN's reservations about a policy or action without necessarily calling for specific remedial action

Approval path:

  • Advocacy staff drafts
  • GPA Chair reviews and approves (or requests revision)
  • Senior Staff reviews and approves (with focus on external communications, legal, and relationship implications)
  • Executive Director is notified and can flag escalation concerns
  • All approvals documented in policy activity log

Timeline: Advocacy staff to GPA Chair (8–12 hours), GPA Chair to Senior Staff (4–8 hours), Senior Staff to Executive Director (4–8 hours). Total: 48–72 hours if all approvals are in place.

Tier 3: Escalated response 3–5 days

When to use: High visibility, significant controversy, major coalition impact, or potential legal or reputational risk. May introduce new SfN positioning or expand the Society's public role.

Examples: Statement on a major federal policy or bill with significant neuroscience implications. Position on a controversial social or political issue where SfN's voice is requested by coalition partners. Public response to criticism of the neuroscience field or SfN itself. Major media statement expected to generate national coverage.

Key characteristics:

  • Expected to receive substantial media coverage (national, international, or major-outlet)
  • Likely to generate member debate, coalition partner input, or public controversy
  • May introduce new or revised SfN position; shifts how SfN is perceived publicly
  • Moderate to significant legal, financial, or reputational risk
  • Multi-stakeholder or multi-coalition implications; alignment is critical
  • Potential to impact fundraising, membership, partner relationships, or SfN's institutional position

Approval path:

  • Advocacy staff drafts with input from GPA Chair
  • GPA Chair reviews and recommends approval or escalation
  • Senior Staff reviews with focus on legal, financial, member, and external relations implications
  • Executive Director reviews and approves in writing (email or recorded message required)
  • GPA Chair confirms readiness to defend statement externally
  • All approvals and decision rationale documented in policy activity log

Timeline: Advocacy staff to GPA Chair (12–24 hours), GPA Chair to Senior Staff (12–24 hours), Senior Staff to Executive Director (24–48 hours), Executive Director decision (24–48 hours). Total: 3–5 days. If timeline pressures shorten this, escalation requires Executive Director approval to proceed.

Use these templates as starting points for rapid response statements. Customize message, evidence, and call-to-action to fit the specific situation and audience.

Template 1: Standard statement Official position
[DATE] [STATEMENT TITLE] [OPENING: State what is happening and why SfN is responding. 1–2 sentences.] [CONTEXT: Explain the issue, its relevance to neuroscience, and why SfN has expertise to comment. Reference prior SfN positions if applicable. 2–3 sentences.] [EVIDENCE: Summarize the scientific basis for SfN's view. Distinguish between what is known, unknown, and evolving. Cite key studies or findings if possible. 2–4 sentences.] [POSITION: Clearly state what SfN believes or is calling for. Use active language. 2–3 sentences.] [CALL TO ACTION: If applicable, specify what SfN is doing, what we are asking of others, or what members should know. 1–2 sentences.] [CLOSING: Reinforce the mission alignment and SfN's commitment. 1 sentence.] For more information: [contact email/spokesperson]
Template 2: Letter to policymakers Policy advocacy
[DATE] The Honorable [NAME] [ADDRESS] RE: [BILL NUMBER AND TITLE] OR [ISSUE/REGULATION] Dear [TITLE NAME]: [OPENING: State the issue and SfN's interest. 1–2 sentences.] [EVIDENCE: Explain the neuroscience evidence relevant to the policy question. Why does this matter? What does the science say? 3–4 sentences.] [POSITION: State SfN's position clearly. What do we support? What do we oppose? 2–3 sentences.] [IMPACT: Describe why this matters to neuroscientists, to the field, or to the public. 2–3 sentences.] [CALL TO ACTION: What specific action is SfN requesting (vote yes, amend, fund, study, etc.)? 1–2 sentences.] [CLOSING: Thank you and offer further assistance.] Sincerely, [SIGNATORY TITLE AND NAME] Society for Neuroscience CC: [Relevant stakeholders]
Template 3: Statement of concern Policy concern
[DATE] SfN Statement of Concern: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF ISSUE] The Society for Neuroscience expresses concern regarding [DESCRIBE THE POLICY, DECISION, OR ACTION]. [CONTEXT: Explain why SfN is concerned about this matter. How does it relate to neuroscience research, the scientific community, or public understanding of brain science? 2–3 sentences.] [EVIDENCE: Present the scientific evidence or rationale supporting SfN's concern. What do we know about the implications of this policy or action? 2–4 sentences.] [POSITION: Clearly articulate SfN's position and the basis for the concern. What aspects are problematic? What would be more appropriate? 2–3 sentences.] [CALL TO ACTION: If applicable, specify what corrective action SfN believes is necessary, or what factors should be considered in reconsidering this policy or decision. If no specific action is being called for, explain what SfN will be monitoring. 1–2 sentences.] [CLOSING: Reinforce SfN's commitment to science, evidence-based policy, or the stated mission. 1 sentence.] For more information: [contact email/spokesperson]
Template 4: Coalition statement Joint position
[DATE] [COALITION NAME] Statement on [ISSUE] [OPENING: Identify the coalition, the issue, and the collective view. 1–2 sentences.] [SIGNATORIES: List all organizations signing the statement.] [CONTEXT: Provide brief context on why this coalition is responding and why the issue matters. 2–3 sentences.] [EVIDENCE/RATIONALE: Summarize the evidence or reasoning supporting the coalition view. 2–4 sentences.] [UNIFIED POSITION: State the collective stance. What do the organizations agree on? 2–3 sentences.] [INDIVIDUAL VIEWS: Optional—brief statement from each organization on why they signed. 1–2 sentences each.] [CALL TO ACTION: What is the coalition asking for? 1–2 sentences.] [CLOSING: Reinforce unity and mission. 1 sentence.] For more information: [Primary contact name and email] [Secondary contacts from key organizations]
Template 5: Holding statement Immediate response
The Society for Neuroscience is aware of [BRIEF EVENT DESCRIPTION]. We are actively assessing the situation and will provide a fuller response in the coming [hours/days]. In the meantime, [OPTIONAL: brief statement of principle or established position]. Contact: [Comms email]

Escalation paths define what happens when the rapid response process encounters a complication: missing approvers, scope creep, legal risk, or a situation that turns out to require more than a rapid response.

Escalation scenarios
SituationActionWho decides
Executive Director is unreachable within required timeframe Designated Senior Staff lead may approve Tier 2 with immediate written notification to Executive Director and GPA Chair. Tier 1 same rule applies if no Tier 2 delay is feasible. Senior Staff lead
GPA Chair is unavailable Any GPA Executive Committee member may serve as reviewer. Document in activity log. Advocacy staff + Senior Staff
Scope expands during drafting (issue is larger than initially assessed) Stop drafting. Return to go/no-go decision with updated assessment. Consider shifting to Tier 3 or issuing a holding statement while standard process is initiated. Executive Director
Legal risk identified (litigation, defamation concern, regulatory compliance) Pause release. Brief Executive Director and Senior Staff immediately. Consult legal counsel before proceeding. Holding statement may be issued in the interim. Executive Director + legal counsel

Coalition partner statement is not fully aligned with SfN's position Do not sign. Notify partner of specific concerns. Consider issuing a standalone statement instead if timing permits. Advocacy staff + GPA Chair
Statement is released and generates significant negative response Convene rapid debrief within 24 hours. Do not issue a retraction without Executive Committee approval. Prepare clarifying statement if warranted. Executive Director + GPA Chair
Media requests comment before statement is approved Provide only the holding statement or a standard "we are assessing the situation" response. Do not improvise on-the-record comments. Refer to designated spokesperson. Comms + Advocacy staff
Post-release debrief agenda

Schedule 30–45 minutes 5–7 days after release with policy staff, GPA Chair, and Senior Staff. Use this agenda:

  1. Did the statement accomplish its intended goal? (Mission advancement, coalition support, member guidance)
  2. Was the process followed as designed? Where did it break down?
  3. Were approval timelines met? If not, why?
  4. How did the statement land externally? (Media, members, coalition partners, policymakers)
  5. Is follow-up action needed? (Additional statement, testimony, advocacy outreach)
  6. What should be updated in this framework based on what we learned?
Framework review cadence
  • This framework should be reviewed annually by policy staff and the GPA Chair.
  • Any rapid response activation should trigger a post-release debrief, the notes from which are used in the next annual review.
  • Backup contacts and approval authority assignments should be confirmed at the start of each program year.
  • Major changes to the framework require Executive Director and GPA Chair approval.
